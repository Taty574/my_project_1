The Internet

Category: Infrastructure & Networking

Definition

The Internet is a global system of interconnected computer networks that use the standard Internet Protocol Suite (TCP/IP) to link several billion devices worldwide.

The "Network of Networks"

The Internet is described as a "Network of Networks." This means it consists of millions of private, public, academic, business, and government networks linked by a broad array of electronic, wireless, and optical networking technologies.

How it Differs from the Web

While people use these terms interchangeably, they are technically different:

  • Infrastructure: The Internet is the hardware (cables, routers, servers).
  • Services: The Internet supports many services, not just the Web. These include Email (SMTP), File Transfer (FTP), and Streaming.

History of the Internet

The Internet did not start as a public service, but as a specialized research project:

  • ARPANET (1969): The Internet began as ARPANET, a project funded by the U.S. Department of Defense. It was designed to allow researchers to share computer resources across long distances.
  • The TCP/IP Breakthrough: In the 1970s, the development of the TCP/IP protocol suite allowed different types of networks to communicate with each other for the first time, creating a true "Network of Networks."
  • Commercialization: By the early 1990s, the Internet moved from being a purely academic and military tool to a commercial platform available to the general public.

Examples of Internet Services

  • Email: Services like Gmail and Outlook rely on the Internet to send and receive messages.
  • File Sharing: Platforms like Dropbox and Google Drive allow users to store and share files over the Internet.
  • Streaming: Services such as Netflix and Spotify use the Internet to deliver content to users.
