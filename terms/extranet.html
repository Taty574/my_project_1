Extranet

Category: Private Networks & Partnerships

Definition

An Extranet is a private network that uses Internet technology and the public telecommunication system to securely share part of a business's information or operations with suppliers, vendors, partners, or customers.

How it Differs from an Intranet

While an Intranet is strictly for internal employees, an Extranet extends the network to authorized outsiders. It acts as a bridge between the public Internet and a private Intranet.

  • Intranet: Employee-only (e.g., internal payroll).
  • Extranet: Partner-accessible (e.g., a supplier checking a company's inventory levels).

Security and Access

Because an Extranet involves external parties, security is the highest priority. It typically requires:

  • Firewalls: To separate the internal network from the external users.
  • VPNs (Virtual Private Networks): To create secure "tunnels" for data to travel.
  • Authentication: Multi-factor login to ensure only specific partners can enter.

Business Use Case

A common example of an Extranet is a large car manufacturer. They may have an Extranet where their tire suppliers can see real-time production schedules to know exactly when to deliver more tires, without the supplier having access to the manufacturer's private HR files.
